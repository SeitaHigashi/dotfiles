# Cap the RTX 3060 Ti power limit for fan noise

- Decided: 2026-09-23
- Module: `modules/gpu.nix` (`nvidia-power-limit.service`)

## Problem

At the stock power limit, the fan ramped to 93% while `bonsai` (27B) inference ran on the
3060 Ti — too loud to run continuously.

## Measurement (2026-09-23)

`bonsai` (27B) held under continuous `llama-server` load, each point held 150–300s to
settle. Fan speed via `nvidia-smi`'s `fan.speed`.

| power limit | actual power | fan | temp | generation tok/s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (none / 200 W default) | 135 W | 93% | 82°C | 29.3 |
| 140 W | 135 W | 94% | 82°C | 29.1 |
| 130 W | 129 W | 92% | 80°C | 28.3 |
| 120 W | 119 W | 79% | 79°C | 27.2 |
| **105 W (chosen)** | 104 W | 60% | 79°C | 25.0 |
| 100 W | 99 W | 54% | 78°C | 23.8 |

**Chosen: 105 W.** Fan drops 93% → 60% for a 15% generation-speed hit. Going to 100 W buys
only 6 more fan points but costs another 1.2 tok/s — not worth it.

## Why watts, not temperature or clock lock

- The card holds a fixed GPU Target Temperature (83°C) via clock speed once above
  ~1350 MHz — temperature stays ~79–82°C regardless of power limit, so a temperature-based
  fan threshold doesn't exist to tune. Fan speed tracks *power dissipated*, roughly
  linear at ~1%/W across the 95–137 W range measured — so power limit is the right control
  variable.
- `nvidia-smi -lgc` (clock lock) was considered and rejected: for the same power draw it
  gives the same noise, but has 20+ points of fan-speed hysteresis after a load spike
  before it settles down, which `-pl` doesn't have. `-pl` also lets light workloads spin
  up to full speed when there's power headroom.
- Above 140 W there is no further effect — temperature (82°C) becomes the limiting factor
  and actual power plateaus at 135 W regardless of the configured ceiling.

## Mechanics

Range is 100.00–200.00 W (1 W steps), default 200 W. Check with
`nvidia-smi -i 1 -q -d POWER`. The setting is volatile even with `nvidia-persistenced`
enabled — it resets on driver reload/reboot — hence a systemd oneshot
(`nvidia-power-limit.service`) that sets it on start and restores 200 W on stop.
